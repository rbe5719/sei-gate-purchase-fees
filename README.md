**3. Fund the account.** Pick one of the methods in the table above. Card payments and SEPA instant transfers typically land within seconds to minutes; other bank transfers can take around a business day or longer depending on your bank.

**4. Buy SEI.** Go to the spot market, search SEI, and open the SEI/USDT pair. You have two realistic choices:

- **Market order** — fills immediately at whatever the best available prices are. Fast, and it counts as a taker order.
- **Limit order** — you name your price and wait. If it rests in the order book instead of filling instantly, it's a maker order and often costs less.

For a first buy, a limit order placed slightly above the current best ask gets you filled quickly while keeping you on the cheaper fee side of the maker/taker split. It's a small habit that pays off if you trade often.

**5. Decide where the SEI lives.** Leaving it on the exchange is simpler and lets you sell instantly. Withdrawing to a self-custody wallet on the Sei network means you control the keys and take on the responsibility that comes with them. If you do withdraw, verify the network and do a small test transfer first — sending to the wrong network is the single most common way people lose tokens permanently.

## Where new buyers actually get tripped up

**Region blocks.** Buying crypto with fiat isn't available everywhere, and payment options don't show up in unsupported regions. That's a compliance rule, not a bug — but it means a friend in another country seeing a card option doesn't guarantee you will.

**Declined cards.** Gate's own FAQ notes that banks sometimes flag crypto purchases as suspicious and reject them. If your card fails, it's usually the bank, not the exchange. P2P or a bank transfer is the fallback.

**Ticker collision.** Before you buy anything labeled SEI, confirm you're looking at the Sei Network token and not the NYSE-listed energy company, and not a lookalike token on another chain. Wallet providers explicitly warn that multiple tokens can share the same name and symbol. On Gate, the spot pair you want is SEI/USDT, reached through the platform's own market listing rather than a link from a forum post.

**Withdrawal network choice.** The Sei network address is not the same as a Solana address or an ERC-20 address. Wrong network, permanently lost funds, no support ticket that fixes it.

**Leverage.** Gate offers margins, leveraged tokens and perpetual futures on many assets. These amplify losses exactly as efficiently as gains, and the "zero fee leveraged products" language in marketing is about trading fees, not about risk. If your goal was to buy SEI and hold some, leverage is a different product with a different failure mode.
